# CALL SHEET — Cold Call, Trades

Rendered as markdown in call mode's left column (D-CRM-28). `{{greeting}}` / `{{reviewLine}}` / `{{niche}}` / `{{city}}` / `{{signoff}}` are swapped for the real lead before rendering (`lib/crm/call-script.ts`) — the "better than anyone else in Langley" line was an example claim, not something to assert live about a real business, so it's gone in favor of a line that's true regardless of who else is in town.

Goal of this call is NOT to sell a website. It's to get a yes to "can I send you the link." That's it. 3 minutes, max.

## Before you dial (10 seconds)

Have in front of you, out loud-ready:

- Their first name and business name
- Their review count and rating ("87 reviews, 4.8")
- One thing a review actually says ("someone called you out by name for fixing a leak in the rain")
- Whether they have no site or a bad/dead site

## 1. Disarm the assumption — 10 sec

They answered because they think you're a customer. Kill that instantly or the whole call sours when it collapses.

> "{{greeting}} I'm not calling for a quote, so I'll be quick. I build websites for trades around the Fraser Valley."

Then keep going. Don't pause here — a pause invites "not interested."

## 2. State the pain as fact, in their numbers — 20 sec

Don't ask how he feels about it. Tell him what's happening, specifically.

> "{{reviewLine}} But when someone searches '{{niche}} {{city}}' and finds you on Maps, there's nowhere for them to go. They tap through to the guy with the website instead. You're losing those jobs and you never even see it happen."

Why this works: it's flattering, it's specific, it's about money, and it frames the problem as external (Google, competitors) rather than as his failure. Nobody defends against a compliment.

## 3. The ONE question — 10 sec

One. Not a discovery interview.

> "Has anyone ever told you they had trouble finding you online?"

He'll say yes, or "yeah, people ask if we have a site." He just stated the pain himself, which is worth ten times you stating it.

If he says no: don't push. Move straight on.

## 4. The ask — 15 sec

This is the actual close of this call.

> "So here's why I called. I build sites for trades like yours — five pages, your reviews on it, your services, looks sharp on a phone. There's one live right now you can look at. Would you rather I text or email you the link?"

Either answer is a yes — and it hands you the address or confirms the number while you're still talking.

## 5. SHUT UP

Silence. Let it land. Whoever speaks first loses.

## 6. If he says yes → close the loop

> "Sending it now. Have a look tonight — I'll follow up tomorrow. If it's not for you, tell me and I'll leave you alone. If you like what you see, I'll walk you through what it takes to get yours."

Send it while you're still on the phone. Then log it and move on. Do not sell price here.

## Price — only when HE asks

He usually will. Say it flat, no flinch, no discount.

> "$1,200 to build it. About $36 a month after that to run it — and that goes straight to the platform, not to me. The account's in your name."

Then immediately, your best card:

> "Which means if you ever want to fire me, you keep everything. Site, domain, customer list. I'm not the web guy who holds your business hostage."

Stop talking again.

## Objections — one line each, then silence

| He says | You say |
|---|---|
| "My nephew's building one" | "How long's that been in progress?" (It's been 18 months. It will never be done. Don't gloat — just: "Yeah. Happens a lot. Want to see mine anyway? Costs you nothing to look.") |
| "I get enough work from word of mouth" | "You clearly do — that's why I called you and not the guy with six reviews. This is for the people who already heard about you and want to check you out before they call." |
| "Not interested" | "No problem. Can I text you the link anyway? If it's junk, delete it." (One try. If he says no again — "All the best{{signoff}}" — and hang up warm. He may call back in three months.) |
| "Too expensive" | "Fair. What's one roof worth to you?" (Silence.) |
| "Send me an email" | "Will do — what's the best address?" (That's verbal consent. Take it.) |
| "Who are you with?" | "My own company — Agora Network Technologies. I build software full-time; this isn't a template shop." |

## Save these for the walkthrough call, not the cold call

Once he's seen the site and you're on a 10-minute follow-up, THEN the deeper pain is fair game — and now it's a conversation, not an ambush:

- "How are you tracking who's called you? Texts and memory?"
- "How long does it usually take you to get back to someone who enquires?"
- "Are you replying to your Google reviews?" (Almost nobody is. Reviews get more reviews.)
- "How are you invoicing right now?"

Each of these opens a door to something the toolkit already does — and this is where the Care Plan sells itself.

## Rules

- **Never lead with your résumé.** The live site is the credential. "Ten years, senior engineer, AI, fast" tells him it's easy and cheap. Let him assume it was hard.
- **Never mention the platform by name on the phone.** It comes up at account creation, where it's a feature, not a shortcut.
- **Never mention the job search or that this is a side hustle.** True, irrelevant, and it makes the $1,200 feel risky.
- **Never discount on the call.** If he balks, remove scope, not price.
- **Talk less than half the time.** Every silence you fill is a yes you talked him out of.
- **Log the outcome the second you hang up.** Called / sent / dead / callback.

Ten dials will teach you more than this page. Update it after the first batch — the objections you actually hear are the ones worth scripting.
