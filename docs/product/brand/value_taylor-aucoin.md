## 1. The outcomes sold (in the buyers' words)

**Hiring manager (Segment 1)**
- **Before:** they want someone "comfortable working without heavy PM overhead" who can "help to shape the problem, not just implement a solution" (Linear). In Taylor's words, they "don't have the right person in the room." Their diagnosis: "senior judgment is now the scarce asset: what to build, what to gate, what to trust to production" (Propel Labs). They give an application "one minute" (PostHog).
- **After:** someone who will "Own features end to end, scope, build, ship, measure, iterate" (Typify), proven by "evidence of building and owning a product" (HN founder post). After a fractional engagement, in Bruno's words: they could "continuously maintain and expand the platform independently without dedicated engineering staff."

**Founder buying a build (Segment 2a)**
- **Before:** "a founder built an MVP with an AI tool like Lovable or Cursor and needs someone accountable for getting it to real users safely" (thenotus).
- **After:** "a real product, not a prototype" (Taylor's site), with "documentation the next engineer inherits."

**Professional buying a coded site (Segment 2b)**
- **Before:** "We couldn't update it ourselves, so it was always old news." · "I do not have to be the only person who has the knowledge to manage our webpage." · The fear: "make absolutely sure that you have control of the web site."
- **After:** "full control over website without having to rely on and pay a web designer," and "I get tons of compliments on my website." Both are buyers praising DIY builders: the after-state they want, not anything said about Taylor.

**Learner (Segment 3)**
- **Before:** "AI solutions that are almost right, but not quite."
- **After:** "a refuge of anti-slop" (a PostHog reader).

## 4. Positioning

For founders and heads of product who need one person to shape the problem and ship the answer, **Taylor Aucoin is a Product Engineer** who takes a product from idea to launch, built to be measured, with his judgment over the AI. **Unlike an engineer who builds what's specified,** he shows the decisions behind twelve products taken from zero to one, and leaves behind conventions a team keeps building on.
